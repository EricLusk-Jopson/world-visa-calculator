Patricia can begin a brand-new 90-day stay in the Schengen Area on **November 25, 2025**.

Reasoning

1. **Length of the Previous Stay:**

- **Entry Date:** March 1, 2025
- **Exit Date:** May 29, 2025
- Under Schengen rules, both the entry day and exit day count as presence in the Schengen Area.
- **Days spent:** 31 days (March) + 30 days (April) + 29 days (May) = **90 days**.
- She used her entire 90-day allowance during this trip.

2. **The 90/180-Day Rolling Rule:**

- Under the rule, on **every single day** of a stay in the Schengen Area, the traveler must not have spent more than 90 days in the Schengen Area within the 180-day period looking back from (and including) that day.
- To complete a **full, continuous 90-day stay**, her entire 90-day previous stay must fall completely outside of the 180-day look-back window calculated from the _end date_ of her new 90-day trip.

3. **Determining the Re-entry Date:**

- To have a full 90-day allowance completely clear from day 1 of the new trip through day 90, the 180-day look-back window on the **last day** of her new trip must not include her exit date from the previous trip (May 29, 2025).
- May 29, 2025 is exactly 180 days prior to **November 24, 2025** (May 29 + 179 days = November 24).
- Starting on **November 25, 2025**, her previous exit date of May 29, 2025 falls 180 days into the past and is completely clear of the look-back window.
- A full 90-day trip starting on **November 25, 2025** will run through **February 22, 2026**. On February 22, 2026, the 180-day look-back window extends back to August 27, 2025—well clear of her May 29 exit, meaning 0 days of prior stay fall within the 180-day window for any day of this new trip.
