Key Calculations & Reasoning

Under the Schengen 90/180-day rule, on every single day of a stay, a traveler must look back **180 days** (including that day) and ensure that the total number of days spent in the Schengen Area does not exceed **90 days**. Both the arrival and departure days count as full calendar days.

1. First Trip Breakdown

- **Dates:** February 1, 2025 – March 22, 2025
- **Days used:** 28 days in February + 22 days in March = **50 days**

2. Determining the Entry Date

Since Anna wants to stay for **60 days**, if any part of her previous 50-day stay falls within the 180-day look-back window, her combined total would exceed 90 days (50 + 60 = 110 days max):

- To have the full **60-day allowance** available continuously throughout her trip, her previous 50-day stay must fully move outside the rolling 180-day window.
- Specifically, by the **last day** of her new 60-day trip, the start date of her previous trip (February 1, 2025) must be more than 180 days in the past.

3. Working Backward from the Window Expiration

1. **Day 180 from Feb 1, 2025:** Counting 180 days starting from February 1, 2025 ends on **July 30, 2025** (Feb: 28, Mar: 31, Apr: 30, May: 31, Jun: 30, Jul: 30).
1. **First day with 0 prior Schengen days in the look-back window:** On **July 31, 2025**, the 180-day look-back window covers February 2, 2025 to July 31, 2025, which means February 1, 2025 is now outside the window.
1. **Calculating the entry date:** If the 60-day trip ends on **September 18, 2025** (Day 60), the 180-day look-back window on Day 60 extends back to March 23, 2025. Because her previous trip ended on March 22, 2025, **0 days** of her previous trip fall into the window.

- **Entry Date:** **July 21, 2025**

On every single day from July 21 to September 18, 2025, her total days spent in the Schengen Area in the preceding 180 days will strictly remain at or below **90 days**.
