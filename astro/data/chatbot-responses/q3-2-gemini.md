The last date Anna is allowed to be present in the Schengen Area is **July 31, 2025**.

Step-by-Step Explanation

Under the Schengen 90/180-day rule, a traveler can spend a maximum of 90 days in the Schengen Area within **any rolling 180-day window**, with both entry and exit days counted as days of stay:

1. **Prior Stay (50 Days):**

- **Entry:** February 1, 2025
- **Exit:** March 22, 2025
- **Calculation:** 28 days in February + 22 days in March = **50 days used**.

2. **Remaining Allowance:**

- Total permitted stay = 90 days.
- Remaining days = 90 - 50 = **40 days**.

3. **New Stay starting May 3, 2025:**

- Anna enters on May 3, 2025, and uses her 40 remaining days consecutively.
- **May 3 – May 31:** 29 days (May has 31 days: 31 - 3 + 1 = 29).
- **June 1 – June 11:** 11 days (29 + 11 = 40 days used).
- By **June 11, 2025**, she exhausts her initial 90-day cap.

4. **The Rolling 180-Day Window Effect:**

- Because the 180-day window looks backward daily, Anna does not have to exit on June 11.
- On **July 31, 2025**, the 180-day look-back window spans from **February 2, 2025, to July 31, 2025**.
- February 1, 2025 (1 day of her previous stay) drops outside the 180-day window, but her previous stay from February 2 to March 22 still contributes **49 days** to the count.
- Combined with her current trip from May 3 to July 31 (**90 days**), her total stay within that 180-day window reaches exactly **139 days total**, but only **90 days fall within the rolling 180-day period** (49 days from prior trip + 41 days from current trip = 90 days).
- On **August 1, 2025**, the 180-day window extends back to **February 3, 2025**. On that day, she would have 48 prior days + 92 new days in the window = **90 days used**, but her new trip alone would reach 91 consecutive days, exceeding the absolute maximum continuous stay allowance without dropping prior days fast enough to stay at or under 90.

Therefore, she can stay for **90 continuous days** starting May 3, making **July 31, 2025**, her required last date of presence.
