The earliest date Jordan can enter the Schengen Area for a 50-day trip without overstaying is **May 26, 2026**.

---

Step-by-Step Explanation

1. Calculation of Previous Days Spent in Schengen

Both the entry and exit days count as full days of stay:

- **Trip 1:** March 14, 2025 – March 21, 2025 = **8 days**
- **Trip 2:** April 18, 2025 – July 2, 2025 = **76 days**
  _(13 days in Apr + 31 in May + 30 in Jun + 2 in Jul)_
- **Trip 3:** September 19, 2025 – September 26, 2025 = **8 days**
- **Trip 4:** November 27, 2025 – December 30, 2025 = **34 days**
  _(4 days in Nov + 30 in Dec)_

---

2. Understanding the Rolling 180-Day Rule

Under EU regulations, on **every single day** of a trip, the traveler must look back at the 180-day window ending on that day. The total number of Schengen days within that 180-day window must never exceed **90 days**.

For a **50-day trip**, the critical point is the last day of the trip (Day 50), because that is when the lookback window covers the maximum amount of recent travel.

---

3. Identifying the Earliest Compliant Entry Date

Let E be the date of entry and L = E + 49 days be the 50th day (last day) of the trip.

- On day L, the 180-day lookback window starts on L - 179 days.
- Trip 4 (34 days) occurred between Nov 27, 2025 and Dec 30, 2025.
- During the new 50-day trip, Jordan will accumulate **50 new days**.
- To stay within the **90-day limit**, Jordan can have at most 90 - 50 = **40 days** from previous trips remaining inside the 180-day lookback window.

Since Trip 4 was 34 days long, as long as the 180-day window on day L drops **Trip 3** (September 19–26, 2025) entirely out of view, the only past trip inside the window will be Trip 4 (34 days). 34 days (Trip 4) + 50 days (New Trip) = 84 days ≤ 90 days.
