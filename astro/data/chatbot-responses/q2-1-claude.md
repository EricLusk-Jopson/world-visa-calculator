**Earliest entry date: August 28, 2025**

**Days already used:** Counting entry and exit days, March 1 to May 29, 2025 is 31 + 30 + 29 = 90 days, so Patricia used her full allowance.

**What the new trip requires:** On every day of the new trip, the days spent in the Schengen Area during the 180-day window ending that day (including that day) must be 90 or fewer.

**The binding day is the last one.** Each day of the new trip adds one day to her count, while old days drop out of the window by at most one per day, so the total never decreases during the trip. It is therefore highest on day 30. On that day she will have 30 new days, so no more than 60 old days can still be inside the window.

**Finding the cutoff:** The 60 most recent old days are March 31 to May 29 (1 + 30 + 29 = 60). So the 180-day window ending on day 30 of the trip must start no earlier than March 31, 2025, which means March 1–30 must have dropped out.

- A window starting on March 31 ends 179 days later, on **September 26, 2025**, which is day 30 of the trip.
- Counting back 29 days from September 26 gives an entry date of **August 28, 2025**.

**Check:** For a trip from August 28 to September 26, the window ending September 26 runs March 31 to September 26. It contains 60 old days plus 30 new days, for exactly 90. Entering a day earlier would put 61 old days in that final window and result in 91 days, an overstay.
