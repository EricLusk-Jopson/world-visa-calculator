**Answer: May 29, 2025**

**Reasoning:**

Under the 90/180 rule, both the day of entry and the day of exit count as full days of presence in the Schengen Area. So if Patricia enters on March 1, that day is Day 1 of her 90 days.

Counting the days of presence, assuming she stays continuously:

- March 1 to March 31: 31 days (running total 31)
- April 1 to April 30: 30 days (running total 61)
- May 1 to May 29: 29 days (running total 90)

Day 90 therefore falls on May 29, 2025. That is the last day she may be present. She must be out of the Schengen Area by the end of that day. If she were still there on May 30, that would be day 91 and an overstay.

**Why the rolling window doesn't change this:** Since she has no prior travel history, nothing before March 1 counts against her. The 180-day look-back window on any given day only contains days from her current continuous stay, so the 90-day cap is reached simply by counting forward.

**Practical note:** Because exit day counts, she should physically cross the border on or before May 29. If she plans to leave that day, she should allow enough time for border processing so she isn't delayed past midnight.
